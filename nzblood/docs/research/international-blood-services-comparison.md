# International Blood Services Comparison

Research comparing blood collection and products across different countries, with focus on differences from New Zealand Blood Service.

---

## Global Context (WHO Data)

### Donation Rates by Country Income Level

| Income Level | Median Donations per 1,000 population |
|--------------|---------------------------------------|
| High-income | 31.5 |
| Upper-middle income | 16.4 |
| Lower-middle income | 6.6 |
| Low-income | 5.0 |

### Key Global Statistics

- ~118.5 million blood donations collected globally per year
- 40% of donations occur in high-income countries (which represent 16% of world population)
- 79 countries collect >90% of supply from voluntary unpaid donors
- 54 countries still rely on family replacement or paid donors for >50% of supply
- Only 56 of 171 reporting countries manufacture plasma-derived products domestically; 91 import all supplies

### Screening Quality Disparity

| Income Level | Donations Screened with Quality Procedures |
|--------------|-------------------------------------------|
| High-income | 99.8% |
| Low-income | 76% |

HIV prevalence in donations: 0.002% (high-income) vs 0.70% (low-income)

## Country Profiles

### Australia - Red Cross Lifeblood

**Organizational Model:** Not-for-profit health service provider

**Scale:**
- ~3,500 staff
- ~1,000 non-donor volunteers
- Network of donor centres across Australia

**Donation Types:**
- Blood (whole blood)
- Plasma (apheresis)
- Platelets
- Breast milk (for neonatal use)
- Faecal microbiota transplantation
- Organ and tissue registration
- Blood stem cells

**Key Difference from NZ:** Australia collects breast milk and faecal microbiota - donation types not offered by NZBS.

**Blood Products:**
- Red cells, platelets, fresh frozen plasma
- Cryodepleted plasma, cryoprecipitate
- Serum eye drops
- Fractionated plasma products

**Unique Programs:**
- Lifeblood Teams (group donation)
- Donate with a Friend program
- Patient blood management resources for health professionals

**History:** 90+ years of blood donation operations

---

### United Kingdom - NHS Blood and Transplant (NHSBT)

**Organizational Model:** NHS entity (government health service)

**Scale:**
- 2 million voluntary blood donations collected annually
- Serves England (Scotland, Wales, Northern Ireland have separate services)

**Donation Types:**
- Whole blood
- Plasma
- Platelets
- Cord blood banking
- Stem cell registry

**Blood Products:**
- Red blood cells (35-day shelf life)
- Platelets (7-day shelf life)
- Plasma (up to 3 years storage)

**Key Difference from NZ:** UK has separate services for different nations. NZ has single national service.

**Eligibility Criteria:**
| Criteria | UK | NZ |
|----------|-----|-----|
| First-time donor age | 17-65 | 16-71 |
| Upper age (existing) | Beyond 72 with regular status | 75 (81 with approval) |
| Minimum weight | 50kg | 50kg |
| Maximum weight | 158kg | Not specified |
| Donation frequency (men) | Every 12 weeks | Similar |
| Donation frequency (women) | Every 16 weeks | Similar |

**Unique Approaches:**
- Black heritage donor recruitment program (Brixton Donor Centre)
- Focus on ethnic minority communities for better blood matching
- Individual health assessment approach

**Permanent Deferrals (UK):**
- Most cancer types
- Blood products received after January 1, 1980
- HIV positive
- Organ transplants
- Hepatitis B or C carriers
- Non-prescribed injectable drug use

### Canada - Canadian Blood Services

**Organizational Model:** Independent registered charity, federally-regulated biologics manufacturer

**Governance:** Established 1998 following Justice Horace Krever's Royal Commission inquiry into blood contamination crisis. Designed to ensure oversight while protecting independence.

**Funding:** Provincial and territorial governments (excluding Quebec - which operates Hema-Quebec separately)

**Key Difference from NZ:** Canada has two separate blood services - CBS and Hema-Quebec. NZ has single national service.

**Donation Types:**
- Blood (whole blood ~450ml)
- Plasma (can donate every 7 days)
- Platelets (every 14 days)
- Cord blood collection
- Adult stem cell registry
- Organ and tissue coordination

**Guiding Values:** Integrity, Collaboration, Adaptability, Respect, Excellence ("Canada's Biological Lifeline")

**Eligibility:**
| Criteria | Canada | NZ |
|----------|--------|-----|
| Minimum age | 17 | 16 |
| Whole blood frequency (male) | Every 56 days | Similar |
| Whole blood frequency (female) | Every 84 days | Similar |
| Plasma frequency | Every 6-14 days | Less frequent |

**Unique Programs:**
- National plasma protein formulary
- Public cord blood bank
- Living kidney donation programs
- Interprovincial organ sharing registry
- Diagnostic laboratory testing (select provinces)

---

### Netherlands - Sanquin

**Organizational Model:** Not-for-profit organization

**Scale:**
- 100,000 patients benefit annually
- 11 departments (restructured 2024)
- Headquarters: Amsterdam

**Governance:**
- Executive Board (day-to-day operations)
- Supervisory Board (accountability)
- ANBI (Public Benefit Organisation) status
- NEN 7510 information security certification

**Key Difference from NZ:** Sanquin operates significant research programs and educational initiatives. More vertically integrated.

**Unique Approaches:**
- Internationally leading blood transfusion research
- Educational projects (e.g., "Minecraft: Operation Aorta")
- Integrated diagnostics and education
- Strong sustainability initiatives

---

### United States - Multiple Providers

**Organizational Model:** Fragmented market with multiple providers

**Key Players:**
- American Red Cross (non-profit, largest collector)
- Vitalant (non-profit)
- OneBlood (non-profit)
- Multiple commercial plasma collection companies

**Key Difference from NZ:** US has competitive market model vs NZ's single national provider. US allows paid plasma donation.

**Collection Model:**
- Mix of fixed donor centres and mobile blood drives
- Red Cross supplies ~40% of US blood
- Commercial plasma centers pay donors (separate from whole blood)

**Paid Plasma:**
- US is world's largest source plasma collector
- Commercial companies compensate plasma donors
- Plasma products exported globally
- Many countries (including NZ, UK, Australia) import US-sourced plasma products

**Regulatory Framework:** FDA regulates blood and plasma as biologics

## Key Structural Differences

### Governance Models Compared

| Country | Model | Single/Multiple Providers |
|---------|-------|---------------------------|
| New Zealand | Crown Entity | Single national |
| Australia | Not-for-profit | Single national |
| UK | NHS (Government) | Separate per nation |
| Canada | Independent charity | Two (CBS + Hema-Quebec) |
| Netherlands | Not-for-profit | Single national |
| USA | Mixed market | Multiple competing |

### Paid vs Voluntary Donation

| Country | Whole Blood | Plasma | Compensation |
|---------|-------------|--------|--------------|
| New Zealand | Voluntary only | Voluntary only | None |
| Australia | Voluntary only | Voluntary only | None |
| UK | Voluntary only | Voluntary only | None |
| Canada | Voluntary only | Voluntary only | None |
| Netherlands | Voluntary only | Voluntary only | None |
| USA | Voluntary | Mix (paid plasma centers) | Plasma: $20-50+ per donation |

WHO recommends voluntary unpaid donation for safety reasons.

## Eligibility Criteria Comparison

### Age Limits

| Country | New Donor Min | New Donor Max | Existing Donor Max |
|---------|---------------|---------------|-------------------|
| New Zealand | 16 | 71 | 75 (81 with approval) |
| UK | 17 | 65 | 72+ (regular donors) |
| Canada | 17 | Not specified | Ongoing assessment |
| Australia | 18 | 70 | Varies |

### MSM (Men who have Sex with Men) Policy Evolution

This has been an area of significant policy change globally:

| Country | Previous Policy | Current Policy |
|---------|-----------------|----------------|
| New Zealand | 12-month deferral | 3-month deferral (Dec 2020), IDA approved 2025 |
| UK | 12-month deferral | Individual risk assessment based on behavior |
| Canada | 12-month deferral | 3-month deferral (2019), moving to behavior-based |
| Australia | 12-month deferral | 3-month deferral |

**IDA (Individualized Donor Assessment):** Evidence-based approach asking same questions of all donors regardless of gender/orientation. NZ approved by Medsafe early 2025, expected implementation 2026.

### vCJD (Mad Cow Disease) Deferrals

| Country | Policy |
|---------|--------|
| New Zealand | Restriction lifted Feb 2024 for UK/France/Ireland residents (1980-1996) |
| UK | Still applies for blood transfusion recipients after Jan 1, 1980 |
| Canada | Varies by specific exposure |

## Blood Products Comparison

### Fresh Products (All Countries Similar)

| Product | Shelf Life | Notes |
|---------|------------|-------|
| Red blood cells | 35 days | Universal |
| Platelets | 7 days | Universal |
| Fresh frozen plasma | 2-3 years | Varies slightly |

### Plasma Fractionation

Most countries rely on imported plasma-derived products, particularly from US commercial plasma.

**NZ Products:**
- Albumex 20 / Alburex NZ
- Biostate (Factor VIII)
- Cryoprecipitate
- Rh(D) Immunoglobulin-VF
- Prothrombinex-VF
- Various immunoglobulins (Privigen, Gamunex, Hizentra)

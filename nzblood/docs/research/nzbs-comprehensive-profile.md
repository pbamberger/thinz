# New Zealand Blood Service (NZBS) - Comprehensive Profile

## Organization Overview

New Zealand Blood Service (NZBS) is a Crown Entity established in 1998 under the New Zealand Public Health and Disability Act 2000. It is the appointed entity pursuant to section 63 of the Human Tissue Act 2008, primarily responsible for functions relating to blood and controlled human substances in New Zealand.

Prior to NZBS, multiple regional blood services operated out of hospitals across the country. The consolidation into a single national service improved coordination, quality control, and supply chain management.

### Legal Classification

- **Crown Entity** under the Crown Entities Act 2004
- **Public Benefit Entity** - primary objective is supporting the New Zealand healthcare community
- Required to give effect to Government policy when directed by the Minister of Health

### Governance

The Minister responsible for NZBS is the **Minister of Health** (Hon Simeon Brown). Associate Ministers of Health are Hon Matt Doocey and Hon Casey Costello.

Under the Pae Ora (Healthy Futures) Act 2022 and Crown Entities Act 2004, the responsible Minister:
- Appoints and removes Board members
- Determines Board member remuneration
- Gives directions to the entity
- Reviews operations and performance
- Requests information from the entity
- Participates in setting strategic direction and targets

## Mission and Core Function

NZBS manages the collection, processing, and supply of:
- Blood and blood components
- Controlled human substances
- Related clinical services

**Safety is the cornerstone of everything NZBS does**, reflected in the quality of blood, blood products, tissues, and services available to patients in New Zealand.

### Operational Scale

- Requires **over 5,000 donations per week** across New Zealand
- Supplies public and private hospitals nationwide
- One blood donation can save up to **three lives**
- Blood donations contribute to making **over 13 products**
- Less than **4% of the eligible population** are blood donors

## Donation Types

### 1. Whole Blood (Allogeneic) Donation

Standard blood donation of approximately 470ml for the national supply. Can be used by anyone in need, anywhere in New Zealand.

### 2. Plasma Donation (Apheresis)

Process draws whole blood, separates plasma using a special machine, and returns red blood cells to the donor. Takes up to 60 minutes. Plasma can be made into **up to 11 lifesaving products**.

### 3. Platelet Donation

Requires special assessment with qualified NZBS team member. Platelets have a short shelf life (7 days) and are critical for certain treatments.

### 4. Paediatric Donations

"Baby donor" program for CMV-negative donors. CMV (Cytomegalovirus) can be severe or fatal in low birth weight infants, so CMV-negative blood is reserved for these patients.

### 5. Bone Marrow Donation

Managed through the **NZ Bone Marrow Donor Registry**. Focus areas:
- Maori and Pacific Islander donors (only ~10,000 on registry vs 25+ million Europeans worldwide)
- Ages 18-35
- Other ethnic minority groups (Indian, Middle Eastern, Asian)

Tissue type matching (HLA system) is hereditary. Only 1 in 3 patients find a match within family, requiring registry searches.

### 6. Hip Bone Donation

During hip replacement surgery, the removed femoral head can be donated. Uses include:
- Children with scoliosis (spinal surgery)
- Children with cancer and non-healing fractures
- Adults having repeat hip replacement
- Adults needing bone grafts

Bone is frozen at -80°C and processed into paste, wedges, or matchsticks for surgical use.

### 7. Tissue Donation

Post-mortem donation of:
- Eye tissue
- Heart valves
- Skin

### 8. Organ Donation

Coordinated with Organ Donation New Zealand (ODNZ).

## Donor Eligibility

### Basic Requirements

- **Age**: 16-71 for new donors
- **Weight**: Minimum 50kg
- **Height/weight**: Matrix requirements for first-time donors aged 16-24
- Must be in good health

### Age Limits (Existing Donors)

- Existing donors: Can donate until 75th birthday
- With Medical Officer approval: May continue until 81st birthday
- Lapsed donors (>2 years since last donation): May donate past 71 with approval

### Key Eligibility Factors

The comprehensive A-Z eligibility criteria covers hundreds of conditions including:

**Common Stand-Down Periods:**
- Tattoos/piercings/cosmetic injectables: 3 months
- Blood transfusion: 3 months
- Dental extraction: 7 days
- Antibiotics: 7 days after completing treatment
- Common cold: Must show signs of recovery
- Pregnancy: Wait as many months as pregnancy length (typically 9 months)

**Permanent Deferrals:**
- Leukaemia or lymphoma (ever)
- Most bleeding disorders
- Active or treatment-requiring autoimmune conditions

**Travel Restrictions:**
- Malaria-endemic areas: Various wait periods
- Chagas disease risk areas: Testing required

### Recent Eligibility Changes

**February 2024:**
- vCJD ("mad cow") restriction lifted for UK/France/Ireland residents (1980-1996)

**November 2021:**
- New donor age limit raised to 71
- Existing donor limit raised to 75 (81 with approval)
- Blood transfusion wait reduced from 12 months to 3 months
- Tattoo/piercing wait reduced from 4 months to 3 months
- Various condition-specific relaxations

**December 2020:**
- MSM (men who have sex with men) deferral reduced from 12 months to 3 months
- PrEP users: 3-month deferral introduced

### Individualized Donor Assessment (IDA)

NZBS received Medsafe approval in early 2025 for individualized donor assessment. This evidence-based approach asks the same questions of every donor regardless of gender, sex, or sexual orientation when assessing STI risk.

- Testing upgraded to 5-NAT (adds Parvovirus B19 and Hepatitis A screening)
- Expected implementation: 2026

## Donation Process

### Before Donating

1. Check eligibility (online quiz or phone)
2. Book appointment (online, app, or phone)
3. Prepare:
   - Drink plenty of fluids (24 hours prior)
   - Eat a proper meal (within few hours)
   - Avoid fatty foods (24 hours prior)
   - Bring valid ID

### What to Bring

- NZBS Donor card (physical or app)
- Valid ID (driver's license, passport, birth certificate, school ID, HANZ 18+ card, firearms license)
- Entertainment for plasma donation (up to 60 minutes)

### During Donation

- Complete questionnaire
- Health screening interview
- Blood pressure and hemoglobin check
- Donation procedure
- Rest in Recovery Lounge (10-15 minutes)

### Post-Donation Care

**Immediately:**
- Rest and avoid excessive arm movement
- Drink 500ml+ water
- Avoid smoking for 2 hours

**Next 6 hours:**
- No rushing or long walks
- No prolonged standing
- No strenuous exercise
- No hazardous activities
- No hot showers/baths

**Next 24 hours:**
- Avoid alcohol

**If concerns arise:**
- Call 0800 SAFE BLOOD (0800 723 325) immediately
- Contact GP or emergency department for urgent medical issues

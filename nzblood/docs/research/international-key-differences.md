# Key Differences: International Blood Services vs New Zealand

Summary of notable differences and strategic observations from international comparison research.

---

## Structural Model Differences

### Single National Provider (NZ Model)

New Zealand, Australia, and Netherlands operate single national blood services.

**Advantages:**
- Unified standards and processes
- Efficient national supply management
- Clear accountability
- No duplication

**Contrast with:**
- **UK:** Separate services for England, Scotland, Wales, Northern Ireland
- **Canada:** Two services (CBS and Hema-Quebec)
- **USA:** Fragmented market with multiple providers

### Government vs Independent Charity

| Country | Model |
|---------|-------|
| NZ | Crown Entity (government) |
| UK | NHS (government) |
| Australia | Not-for-profit (independent) |
| Canada | Independent charity |
| Netherlands | Not-for-profit (independent) |

NZ's Crown Entity status provides government oversight but requires ministerial accountability - different balance than independent charity models.

---

## Donation Types NZ Does Not Offer

### Breast Milk Collection (Australia)
Australian Lifeblood collects donated breast milk for premature/sick infants. NZ does not have this program through NZBS.

### Faecal Microbiota Transplantation (Australia)
Australia collects faecal microbiota for FMT procedures. NZ does not offer this through NZBS.

### Cord Blood Banking (UK, Canada)
UK and Canada operate public cord blood banks through their blood services. NZ does not have public cord blood banking through NZBS.

## Eligibility Policy Differences

### Age Limits - NZ More Inclusive

| Criteria | NZ | UK | Canada |
|----------|-----|-----|--------|
| First-time minimum | 16 | 17 | 17 |
| First-time maximum | 71 | 65 | Not specified |

NZ accepts younger donors (16) and older new donors (71) than UK.

### MSM Policy - NZ Moving to Individualized Assessment

All countries have moved away from lifetime MSM deferrals toward risk-based assessment:

- **NZ:** 3-month deferral (2020), IDA approved 2025, implementation 2026
- **UK:** Individual behavior-based assessment
- **Canada:** 3-month deferral, moving to behavior-based
- **Australia:** 3-month deferral

NZ's IDA approach (asking same questions of all donors regardless of gender/orientation) aligns with UK direction.

### vCJD Policy - NZ Ahead

NZ lifted vCJD restrictions for UK/France/Ireland residents (1980-1996) in February 2024. UK still maintains restrictions for blood recipients after January 1, 1980.

## Paid vs Voluntary Donation

### Global Position

All comparable countries (NZ, Australia, UK, Canada, Netherlands) maintain voluntary unpaid whole blood donation.

**USA Exception:** Commercial plasma collection centers compensate donors ($20-50+ per donation). US supplies significant portion of global plasma products.

### Implications

- NZ imports some plasma-derived products manufactured from US paid-donor plasma
- WHO recommends voluntary unpaid donation for safety
- Paid plasma debate ongoing in several countries

## Donor Recruitment Approaches

### Ethnic Community Targeting

| Country | Focus Area |
|---------|------------|
| NZ | Maori and Pacific Islander focus (bone marrow registry emphasis) |
| UK | Black heritage program (Brixton Donor Centre) |
| Canada | Various ethnic communities |
| Australia | Various ethnic communities |

UK has dedicated infrastructure (Brixton centre) for Black heritage donors. NZ focuses on Maori/Pacific but without dedicated facilities.

### Group Donation Programs

All countries have group donation programs:
- NZ: Team Red, Team Gold
- Australia: Lifeblood Teams
- UK: Group booking
- Canada: Various

## Unique International Approaches

### Netherlands - Research Integration
Sanquin operates internationally recognized blood transfusion research programs integrated with collection operations. More vertically integrated than NZ approach.

### UK - Dedicated Community Centres
Brixton Donor Centre specifically designed for Black heritage community. Reported 50,000+ lives saved/improved in first year.

### Australia - Expanded Donation Types
Breast milk and faecal microbiota collection expands concept of "blood service" beyond traditional scope.

### Canada - Separate Provincial Service
Quebec operates Hema-Quebec independently. Allows regional variation but creates complexity.

## Donation Frequency Comparison

### Plasma Donation Frequency

| Country | Plasma Frequency |
|---------|-----------------|
| Canada | Every 6-14 days |
| NZ | Less frequent |

Canada allows more frequent plasma donation than NZ (every 6-14 days vs less frequent in NZ).

### Blood Donation Frequency

Most countries similar: ~8-12 weeks between whole blood donations.

## Technology and Testing

### NAT Testing Evolution

NZ upgrading to 5-NAT (adds Parvovirus B19 and Hepatitis A screening) as part of IDA implementation. Testing standards generally similar across high-income countries.

### Digital Platforms

All comparable countries offer:
- Mobile apps
- Online booking
- Donation tracking
- Usage notifications

NZ's donor app functionality comparable to international peers.

---

## Key Strategic Observations

1. **NZ's Crown Entity model provides strong accountability** but differs from independent charity models used elsewhere

2. **NZ eligibility criteria are relatively inclusive** (younger minimum age, higher maximum age than UK)

3. **NZ does not collect some donation types** available elsewhere (breast milk, faecal microbiota, cord blood)

4. **IDA implementation positions NZ alongside UK** in evidence-based, non-discriminatory eligibility assessment

5. **Ethnic community outreach exists** but without dedicated infrastructure like UK's Brixton centre

6. **Plasma frequency limits in NZ** may be more conservative than Canada

7. **All comparable countries maintain voluntary unpaid donation** - NZ aligned with international best practice

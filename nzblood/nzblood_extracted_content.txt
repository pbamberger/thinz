About
NZBS
New Zealand Blood Service (NZBS) was established in 1998 under the New Zealand Public Health and Disability Act 2000 and is an appointed entity pursuant to section 63 of the Human Tissue Act 2008 being primarily responsible for the performance of functions in relation to blood and controlled human substances in New Zealand. Before this time, there were a number of regional blood services working out of hospitals.
NZBS is a Crown Entity under the Crown Entities Act 2004.  Pursuant to section 7 of the Crown Entities Act 2004, NZBS is required to give effect to Government policy when directed by the responsible Minister, the Minister of Health.
NZBS is also classified a Public Benefit Entity as its primary objective is to support the New Zealand healthcare community through managing the collection, processing and supply of blood, controlled human substances and related services. Safety is the cornerstone of everything NZBS does and this is reflected in the quality of blood, blood products, tissues and services available to patients in New Zealand.
Ministers relevant to NZBS
The Minister responsible for New Zealand Blood Service is the Minister of Health,
Hon Simeon Brown

Saving a Life Starts When You Book
Find out why appointments are important
NZBS works to ensure that every blood donation goes to a patient in need,
and this requires careful planning and forecasting for what the demand from the hospitals will be.
This includes looking carefully each day at the number of donors
who have made appointments for the week by blood type.
Blood type is very important, as this corresponds to the blood types of the patients being treated in hospitals.

Please keep in mind that both flying & donating can dehydrate. Dehydration can make the donor feel ill, possibly faint, and can potentially increase the risk of DVT. Risk is minimised by ensuring there is a 24 hour wait between long haul flights and donating - this does however remain the donors personal choice.

There is more than one way to donate blood.
Continue reading to find out more.
Allogeneic Donations
Allogeneic donations commonly referred to as whole blood donations, is the process of voluntarily donating a unit of blood (approximately 470 ml) for the national supply. This could be used by anyone in need, anywhere in New Zealand.
Book to Donate Blood
Apheresis Donations
Apheresis, pronounced 'ay-fur-ee-sis', is a Greek word meaning 'to take away' or 'to separate'. The process of apheresis involves drawing whole blood from the donor using a special machine, separating the plasma or the platelets, and returning the red blood cell component back to the donor's body.

For many patients with serious blood disorders, a bone marrow transplant
is the best and only hope for a cure.
You can read more about bone marrow transplants at the
New Zealand Bone Marrow Donor Registry
website.
Bone Marrow Donations
Each year, many New Zealanders are diagnosed with leukaemia or other serious blood disorders. These diseases can prevent a patient's blood cells from maturing and functioning correctly. For many of these patients, a bone marrow transplant is the best and only hope for a cure.

If you are having a hip replacement surgery, you can donate your hip (femoral head) bone to be used in treatments to help New Zealanders in need.
Save a Life While Having Hip Surgery
Your donated hip bone could be helping:
children with scoliosis having spinal surgery
children with cancer who have bone fractures that won't heal
adults having repeat hip replacement surgery
adults needing bone grafts for spinal or other orthopaedic surgery

New Zealand Blood Service acknowledges that gender-based sexual activity rules, while necessary to ensure a safe blood supply in the past, have contributed to the stigma faced by men who have sex with men (MSM) in New Zealand.
We are committed to change and working towards introducing an individual
donor
assessment (IDA) when screening potential blood and plasma donors – one that is more inclusive and will enable those men in single-partner relationships with other men to donate.

One donation can save up to three lives!
When you give blood, you're giving a priceless gift that can't be found anywhere else.
Your blood donation can be used in treatments for those going through cancer, undergoing surgery, or who have been in a traumatic accident. It can also be used to help those with autoimmune disorders or bleeding disorders.
By choosing to be a blood donor, you're making a lifesaving difference in communities across New Zealand.

When you join Team Red or Team Gold, you're in great company!
and
give you the opportunity to team up and help save lives by donating blood or plasma with your friends, family members, team mates or work colleagues.
Group giving is open to any group of three or more people who want to do something amazing and contribute to their community. Your group could be a sports club, a church group, a family or a workplace.
We know people have busy lives. That’s why we want to make it easy to fit donations in around your schedule. We can provide a complimentary shuttle service in main cities to pick you up, or set up a blood drive to come to you if you can get enough people to join.
Saving lives feels amazing, but joining Team Red or Team Gold and supporting your local community has other benefits too — like improving the reputation of your club, your group or your organisation – and giving everyone a sense of pride.

Every week, we need over 5,000 donations around New Zealand to supply public and private hospitals across the country.
The demand for blood is constant
Every week, we need over 5,000 donations around New Zealand to supply public and private hospitals across the country. Therefore it's important to understand what type of blood is needed, how much is needed and where it needs to go.
By monitoring donor appointments by blood group and reviewing demand in hospitals daily we can forecast what we need and contact donors to book in to donate, so we always have a constant blood supply.  This is why we ask our donors to make an appointment, as it helps us know what our blood supply will look like in the coming days and weeks.
Sometimes there may be many transfusions scheduled in a short period, so the demand for certain blood types may increase. If this happens, you may get a phone call, e-mail or text from our team, asking you to donate as your blood type is in need. This does mean some donors (typically with common blood types) will be called regularly, while others may not hear from NZBS for several months. But rest assured, when we do contact you it means your blood type is in need.

Am I Eligible to Become a Donor?
If you are thinking of donating for the first time, please check that you meet the height and weight criteria. This is one of our eligibility criteria that help determine if you can donate safely.  The table below indicates donor eligibility based on the required age, height and weight criteria. Further eligibility still applies – please take our Eligibility Quiz to find out more.
If you don't meet the height and weight criteria, there are other ways you can help. Check out our volunteer page to see what you can do.
If you're unsure about your eligibility, please call 0800 GIVE BLOOD (0800 448 325) and ask to speak with a nurse to determine your eligibility to donate.

29 February 2024: Changes to Donor Eligibility Criteria
As of Thursday 29 February 2024, the ‘mad cow’ (vCJD disease) blood donation criteria restriction in New Zealand was lifted. If you lived in the United Kingdom, France or Republic of Ireland between 1980 and 1996 for six months or more, you can now book an appointment to donate blood or plasma and start your lifesaving journey.
Before you book
You will still need to check your eligibility to donate. That’s because there may be other factors that mean you can’t give blood or much needed plasma right now. Our basic online eligibility quiz can be found

Your blood holds amazing lifesaving properties. It's probably something you've never given much thought about. But to the thousands of recipients blood donations save every year, it's priceless.
Find out the different ways we can use your donation.
Fresh blood products
Red Blood Cells (shelf-life: 35 days)
Red Cell Components carry oxygen around the body. Red blood cell transfusions are used to treat patients with chronic anaemia (from disorders such as kidney failure or cancer) and acute blood loss (as a result of trauma or surgery).
Platelets (shelf-life: 7 days)
Platelets have special blood clotting properties and can also be used to control bleeding following cardiac surgery and trauma. They can be used to treat patients undergoing some treatments for blood diseases and cancer.

A little unsure of what to expect during your first donation? Keep reading the information below to learn about what to expect at your appointment.
What to bring to your appointment
Giving blood is a great way to give back to your community and to help thousands of New Zealanders in need. However, you might be a little unsure of what to expect during your first donation. Keep reading the information below to learn about what to expect at your appointment.
Your ID
This can be your NZBS Donor card on your app, Blood Donor ID card, a valid driver's license, a passport, a birth certificate, a school or university ID card, a HANZ 18+ card or a firearm's license
Something to pass the time
This can be a book to read, or use our free Wi-Fi to listen to some music or watch a movie while you give a plasma donation.

Before you donate...
Do you have your ID on you?
You can use your virtual NZBS Donor ID Card (on the NZBS app), your physical NZBS Donor ID Card, your driver's license, passport or birth certificate.
Are you hydrated?
Drinking plenty of fluids 24 hours leading up to your appointment will help reduce the possibility of any after-effects of donating (such as fainting). It will also assist in better blood flow while giving blood or plasma. And if you’d like to make a positive impact on both the environment and saving lives, you’re welcome to bring your own keep cups and drink bottles – helping to reduce waste.
